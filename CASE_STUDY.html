Does It Know It's the Same Song?

The encoder is meant to be a fingerprint. Handed four pairs of real songs — two versions of one track, two tracks by one artist, two of the same era, and two unrelated pieces — it turns out to rank artist above song.

01

The question, and how it was asked

The model's job is to be a Shazam-style fingerprint: any 5-second window of a track should map near its other windows, robustly to noise, EQ and compression. So the obvious test is to hand it two songs by name and ask how similar it thinks they are. 

python -m music_encoding.compare_songs \
    --a "Radiohead - Creep" --b "Radiohead - Creep (Acoustic)"

That resolves each name to a 30-second preview (Deezer, with iTunes as fallback), downloads it, runs it through the encoder, and reports the cosine similarity of the two embeddings — and where that number sits in the corpus's own distribution. That last part is not decoration; without it the number is uninterpretable.

02

Four pairs, and the null they live in

Random pairs of corpus tracks sit at raw 0.943 ± 0.020 and whitened 0.000 ± 0.133. Read the raw column against that: an unrelated pair scores 0.92, and two versions of the same song score 0.958 — every pair inside about one and a third standard deviations of a random pairing.

Quoting a raw cosine of “0.96” as evidence of similarity would be reporting noise. The whitened space does separate the pairs — against a null centred on zero — and the ordering is sensible.

03

The uncomfortable part

Two different Radiohead songs score higher than two versions of the same song.

0.3495 vs 0.2524 — in both spaces, and by a wider margin than separates “same song” from “unrelated” in the raw space.

The embedding is grouping by artist and production style, not by song identity. Karma Police shares Radiohead's recording signature; Creep (Acoustic) is the same composition re-arranged with different instrumentation and tempo. For a fingerprinting product that is the wrong signal: it would prefer a stylistic match by the same act over the actual recording you played.

This is consistent with the quantitative evaluation rather than contradicting it. Over all 163,915 windows, retrieval top-1 is 0.673 (top-10 0.793, chance 2.4e-05): the right track is usually findable, but a third of queries still return the wrong track first, and the ranking is evidently driven more by timbre than by identity. Even the strongest pair here reaches only z = +2.6 — a fingerprint worth shipping would want z ≫ 3.

04

Why the numbers can be trusted

The model never saw raw audio: it was trained on MTG-Jamendo's precomputed 96-band log-mels (merged to 64 bands), and MTG's STFT parameters are undocumented. So embedding a downloaded preview means reproducing a front end we did not have — and getting it wrong would make every number above a measurement of our preprocessing rather than of the model.

Ground truth made it checkable

The MTG audio tarball holds tracks whose .npy we also have, so the same track can be embedded from audio and from MTG's own mel, and the two compared.

The answer is librosa with essentially all defaults. norm='slaney' was the missing piece — torchaudio's MelSpectrogram defaults to norm=None, which costs 0.047. Two incidental findings: the tarball files are full tracks, not 30-second previews despite the raw_30s name, and the frame rate is 46.875 fps (24000/512), not the 43.07 fps of the repo's own waveform path.

So every preview cosine carries a known ~1.3% preprocessing error. Decimals beyond ~0.01 are noise, which is why the percentile columns carry the argument rather than the raw cosines. A second prerequisite was verified rather than assumed: the whitening transform is not stored, but it is reproducible from tracks_raw alone (to a max diff of 1e-5), so query embeddings can be whitened consistently with the corpus.

05

Reproducing it

# by name (Deezer search, iTunes fallback)
python -m music_encoding.compare_songs --a "Radiohead - Creep" \
    --b "Radiohead - Creep (Acoustic)"

# exactly, by ISRC — Deezer's search returns the ISRC, so this is unambiguous
python -m music_encoding.compare_songs --a-isrc GBAYE9200070 --b "Portishead - Roads"

The tool also reports each song's nearest corpus tracks, which is where it is most useful in practice — it shows what the model thinks the song sounds like.

06

What this is not

  • n = 4. An illustration, not an evaluation. The quantitative evidence is the retrieval metric over 163,915 windows; this study explains why that metric matters, and should not be quoted as a benchmark.
  • 30-second previews are usually the hook, not a representative sample of a track, and they are mastered differently from the corpus audio — the two Creep previews differ by 18 dB in median level. The embedding is not loudness-invariant by construction.
  • Two different populations. The previews are mainstream commercial music; the corpus is largely Creative Commons and indie. The tool therefore also reports a query-vs-corpus null, which is the fairer yardstick (it agreed closely here: 0.946 ± 0.016).
  • Spotify cannot be used at all. It removed 30-second previews from the Web API on 2024-11-27 with no scope to re-enable them for a new app, so a Spotify track id cannot be resolved to audio. Deezer and iTunes are free and unauthenticated.
  • Previews are fetched at request time for analysis only and never stored or redistributed; both providers' terms restrict caching audio.
07

What would move the needle

The failure mode is that the representation encodes style more than identity. Three directions, in rough order of promise:

  1. More steps. The step lever was still paying at 128k — retrieval ran 0.345 → 0.673 across a day of training — so this is the proven one.
  2. Instance discrimination. Barlow Twins pulls two views of one track together and decorrelates dimensions, but never pushes different tracks apart — exactly the signal that would separate “same song” from “same artist”. A contrastive term was implemented and tested; at weight 30 it proved to be a reparameterization equivalent to free whitening, so it needs a larger weight or hard-negative mining to bite.
  3. A bigger embedding (128 → 256), which the style-versus-identity confusion gives a concrete reason to try.
